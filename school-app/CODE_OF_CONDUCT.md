# Code of Conduct

Athar is built for teachers and the children they teach. The people who help come from classrooms, school offices and software teams, and many are new to open source. We want all of them to feel welcome.

## How we treat each other

- Be respectful and patient, most of all with teachers and newcomers, who may not know the tools or the words we use. No question is too simple.
- Criticise the work, not the person. Say what is wrong with a change and how it could be better.
- Assume good intent, and ask before you assume the worst.
- Write plainly. Many readers use English or Arabic as a second language.

## What is not accepted

- Harassment, insults, threats or unwanted personal attention.
- Discrimination or demeaning remarks about anyone's gender, religion, nationality, ethnicity, age, disability, language or background.
- Sharing someone's private information without their consent.

## Protecting children

Never post real learner data, or anything that could identify a child, anywhere in the project's spaces: issues, pull requests, discussions, code, tests, screenshots or attached files. A name, a photo, a class list or a real mark sheet all count. Use the made-up school for every example. If you see such data, do not copy or quote it. Report it straight away.

## Reporting a problem

Report privately to the maintainers. Open a private security advisory on the GitHub repository (Security, then Report a vulnerability), or contact a maintainer directly on GitHub. Say what happened and where. We keep reports confidential.

## What maintainers may do

Maintainers may edit or remove any contribution or comment that breaks this code. They may warn a person, and may block a person from the project for a time or for good. Content that identifies a child is removed at once.
