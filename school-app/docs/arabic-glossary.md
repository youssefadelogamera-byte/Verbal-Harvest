# Athar: Arabic wording rules (read fully before translating)

Athar is a school app for Egyptian government schools, grades 4 to 8 (الصف الرابع الابتدائي to الصف الثاني الإعدادي).
The readers are Egyptian teachers. The owner asked for **Egyptian Arabic**.

## Register

- **Interface text (buttons, labels, help lines, messages): light Egyptian Arabic**, the way good Egyptian apps
  write: friendly, short, clear. Use Egyptian forms where they are natural: اختار، ضيف، احفظ، ارفع، امسح، دوس،
  لسه، مفيش، دلوقتي، عشان، إيه، هنا، كده، اللي، ده/دي، محتاج، ممكن، لازم، مش. Keep it respectful and
  professional: no slang, no jokes, no Franco-Arabic, no emoji. If a fully standard word is what an Egyptian
  teacher would expect on a screen (حفظ، إلغاء، تسجيل الدخول، كلمة السر), use it.
- **Curriculum words (topic names, strand names, subject names): the official Arabic of Egyptian school
  books** (Modern Standard Arabic), exactly as a teacher sees them in the ministry book. Never dialect here.
- **Formal statements about a learner (findings, explanations of evidence): simple, clear Modern Standard
  Arabic**, short sentences, no dialect. These go on a child's record.
- Never label or rank a child. Describe records and patterns, not the person. "النتائج في ... منخفضة" not
  "التلميذ ضعيف".
- Use Arabic punctuation (، ؛ ؟). Keep numbers as Western digits (0-9), which is what Egyptian screens use.
  No tatweel. No diacritics unless a word is ambiguous without them. No em dashes or en dashes.
- Gender: write for a mixed audience. Prefer forms that avoid gender (المدرس as a general word is fine;
  imperative in masculine singular is the normal app convention in Egypt).
