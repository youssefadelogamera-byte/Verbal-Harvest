# Athar: Arabic wording rules (read fully before translating)

This copy of Athar is for Learning International Schools in Saudi Arabia, a Cambridge school (Years 1 to 11)
that teaches with Marshall Cavendish books. The readers are teachers and parents in Saudi Arabia, so every Arabic
text is **Modern Standard Arabic**. No Egyptian or other dialect anywhere.

## Register

- **Interface text (buttons, labels, help lines, messages): plain, short Modern Standard Arabic**, the way good
  Saudi apps write: friendly, clear, respectful. Imperatives such as اختر، أضف، احفظ، ارفع، احذف، انقر. No slang,
  no jokes, no Franco-Arabic, no emoji. Words to avoid because they are dialect: مفيش، دلوقتي، عشان، إيه، كده،
  اللي، ده، دي، مش، لسه، ضيف، دوس، اختار (as an imperative).
- **Curriculum words (topic names, strand names, subject names): standard school Arabic**, as an Arabic edition of
  the Cambridge or Marshall Cavendish book would write them. Never dialect.
- **Formal statements about a learner (findings, explanations of evidence): simple, clear Modern Standard
  Arabic**, short sentences. These go on a child's record.
- Never label or rank a child. Describe records and patterns, not the person. "النتائج في ... منخفضة" not
  "الطالب ضعيف".
- Use Arabic punctuation (، ؛ ؟). Keep numbers as Western digits (0-9).
  No tatweel. No diacritics unless a word is ambiguous without them. No em dashes or en dashes.
- Gender: write for a mixed audience. The imperative in masculine singular is the normal app convention.
